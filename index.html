02 agent-architecture

Loop Engineering vs Graph Engineering

Shen Sean Chen — Sean's AI Stories · Talk explainer, 21m31s · published 2026-08-02 · added 2026-08-11

Two buzzwords, taken slowly. The five-rung ladder — prompt → context → skills → loop → graph — where each rung exists because the one below it ran out of road. Loops and graphs aren't competitors: a graph wraps loops, buys speed and predictability rather than intelligence, and needs guardrails precisely because its nodes and its routing stopped being deterministic.

04 evaluation-and-observability

Verification Debt: Guide, Verify, Solve

Anirban Chatterjee — Sonar · AI Engineer World's Fair 2026, Track 8 · Talk explainer, 22m31s · added 2026-08-11

The productivity spike from AI coding tools expires in about three months; the static-analysis warnings and complexity it leaves behind do not. That residue is verification debt, and it scales with criticality — an agent's default quality is flat while the quality a project needs rises. Human review is a leaky backstop: people follow confidently wrong AI advice 79.8% of the time. The criterion worth keeping is one line — verify with a different methodology than the one that generated the code, because a model grading its own output inherits its own blind spots. A vendor talk, and the entry says so.

03How an entry is built

Every entry follows the same shape, so reading the second one is faster than reading the first:

  • TL;DR first — the whole argument in five to seven points. Stop there and you still have the thesis.
  • Vocabulary before jargon — the handful of terms you need, defined in the source's own sense.
  • Diagrams redrawn as inline SVG — never screenshots. They stay sharp, searchable, and free of redistributed slide imagery.
  • Honest about the unresolved — where the source says "we don't know yet", the explainer says so too.
  • A way back to the original — deep-linked timestamps for talks, section references for papers.

Want to add one? The conventions and the step-by-step are in CONTRIBUTING.md, and there are ready-made skeletons in templates/.

Summaries and diagrams here are original work. All credit for the underlying ideas belongs to the original authors, who are named and linked on every entry. If you are an author and want an entry changed or removed, open an issue on the repository.
